The rail, five ways

Everything that is not enclosure is identical in all five: the same rail, the same one-row-per-source definitions, the same tabbed culture block, the same compact rules, the same content. What changes is how a region is held — so what you are choosing between here is a grammar, not a layout.

They are laid out along one ladder, lightest first: whitespace, then dividers, then background, then wells, then cards. R3 is the odd one out on purpose — it puts a different rung under each kind of content instead of one rung under everything.

VariationSeparationHeavyLightOpened

R1 · Dividers

Hairlines and whitespace, nothing boxed. The lightest separation that works, and the one you have seen.

R2 · Panels

Every region is a card of its own on the page's canvas. The strongest separation here, and the most furniture.

R3 · Tiers

The enclosure says what kind of thing it holds: authored entries are gilded, reference sources are plain rows, everything found sits in a well.

 one rung per kind of content love nepotism expanded

R4 · Bands

No borders anywhere. A region is a stretch of background that bleeds past the gutter, and the edge between two backgrounds is the separation.

 background alone love nepotism expanded

R5 · Slabs

Every region carries its own header, and the header pins under the navbar while you are inside that region. No jump bar: the page says where you are instead.

 wells love nepotism expanded

Add ?theme=dark to any of them. The earlier three whole-page layouts are on the first contact sheet.
