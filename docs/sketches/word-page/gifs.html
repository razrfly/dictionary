GIFs, three ways

What may not change in any of them: the search runs in the browser, nothing is written to the database or to browser storage, the media is never rehosted, the search stays G-rated, and the Powered by GIPHY mark is visible. The hook's URL guards, three-page cap and ten-second timeout are untouched. All three sit inside the culture block with the shelf chrome the other four shelves already have.

A · The shared chrome

Heading, byline, one About, one Load more, the shelf's own column width and type scale. Play becomes an overlay on the still instead of a text button under it. Nothing about motion changes: a frame animates when its control is pressed.

The playback question, resolved

#111 L4 says a GIF plays while in the viewport; the three versions above say plays on hover, focus or tap. They are different pages and the choice has to be made rather than inherited.

In-viewport playback

An IntersectionObserver swaps still → animated as the shelf scrolls into view. The reader sees the shelf working without doing anything.

It also fetches every animated rendition on the shelf whether or not anyone wanted one — twelve requests to GIPHY's media hosts per page view, against a key that allows a hundred an hour for everyone — and puts twelve looping animations beside the reading matter. Reduced-motion readers get a shelf that never works at all rather than one they can operate.

Hover, focus or tap · recommended

The animated rendition is fetched when the reader reaches for it, and one frame moves at a time. Keyboard focus is the same gesture, so the control is not a mouse affordance with a button bolted on.

Reduced motion is then a real state rather than an absence: the swap does not happen, the Play control stays, and a reader who wants the animation can still have it deliberately. This is what the version B frames above actually do — hover one.
