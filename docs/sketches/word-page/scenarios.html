Using the space

Three scenarios built on the Oatmeal kit's own vocabulary rather than ours: its half-and-half label/content grid instead of a narrow sidebar, its accordion instead of nine independent disclosures, and its stat tile for the three facts this page can count before it renders.

The change that matters is <details name> — the HTML exclusive-accordion attribute. One source opens at a time, so Johnson's noun and his verb can no longer both be a page long at once, and all nine sources are visible as condensed rows whichever one is open. No JavaScript.

O1 · The wide rail

R5's slabs with the rail sized as a column rather than a margin — 20rem at lg, 24rem above it. Everything the page knows the size of moves into it: the counts as tiles, the source index as quick links, the related words, the thing. The stream holds only what a source decides the size of.

O2 · Split rows

The kit's half-and-half grid applied inside a row rather than around a section: who said it pinned in one column, what they said in the other. This is what fills the width a 68ch prose line will not use.

O3 · Masthead

No rail at all. The kit's section rhythm instead — the word as a full-width masthead, a sticky jump bar, then each region as a label/content section under a full-bleed rule. Here because “use the space” has an answer that is not a wider rail.
