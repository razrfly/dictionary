## Sources used

*The Devil's Dictionary*, [Project Gutenberg eBook #972](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/972):
released 1 July 1997, most recently updated 23 February 2026, produced by
Aloysius and David Widger. Gutenberg's summary says the work was published in
complete form in 1911. The record does not name the printing it transcribes,
so the dossier does not claim one. The Author's Preface says the work ran in a
weekly paper from 1881 until 1906, and that a large part of it appeared in
1906 as *The Cynic's Word Book*. This is the
same Gutenberg text as the project's own Bierce source records
(`priv/replay/bierce.jsonl.gz`).

## Documented, and our interpretation

Documented: the six entries' exact text and headwords, and the authority
records above. Bierce's definitions are satire in his own voice. Quoting one
accurately does not make it a factual statement (#203).

Our interpretation: the lens "hypocrisy, self-interest and precise satirical
contrast" (#275 §4), the selection rule, the principles, the blind spots and
the two examples. They are a lens we propose, not a reconstruction of Bierce's
beliefs or a claim about what he would choose. The work also contains period
prejudice. INDISCRETION, on women, is cited as one documented example, and the
lens excludes such attitudes.

## Selection hypothesis to test

**Hypocrisy, self-interest and precise satirical contrast.** The arm should
prefer items whose own source shows a professed principle beside the conduct
or interest that contradicts it. It should prefer the exact contrast to the
loud one, and should abstain when no item documents such a gap. On the same
frozen packets as the chosen baseline, scorecard v2 should show a supported
rate no lower than the baseline's and no more unsupported reasons. The lens
has failed if it rewards sneering at groups, calls a person a hypocrite in
the dictionary's voice, or favours items for echoing the Bierce lead already
on the page.
