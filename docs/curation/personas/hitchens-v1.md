## Rights

Hitchens died in 2011, and both works are in copyright. The captured Slate page
carries "©2003 Microsoft Corporation"; the Vanity Fair page carries "© 2026
Condé Nast". The dossier keeps five short quotations, 27 and 26 words from the
two works, for attribution and commentary as fair use. Beyond the two titles
and the pages' copyright notices, it reproduces nothing else: the locators
describe paragraphs (by number for Slate, by subject for Vanity Fair) rather
than quote their openings. A public profile page should show no more than
these quotations. Each source's `rights` field repeats this.

## Documented, and our interpretation

Documented: the five quoted phrases with their dates and locators. The Slate
column's appended correction of 21 October 2003, about what Mother Teresa
said in her Nobel Peace Prize lecture, documents one overstated claim. The
current Slate republication
([slate.com](https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2003/10/the-fanatic-fraudulent-mother-teresa.html))
keeps ¶2 and ¶7 but omits ¶8. That is why ¶8 is cited from the 2003 capture.

Our interpretation: the selection rule, the principles, the blind spots and the
two examples. They are a lens we propose, not a reconstruction of Hitchens's
beliefs or a claim about what he would choose. The lens takes no position on
the subjects of either essay (religion, the beatification, interrogation
policy). It also carries none of his political positions, which changed over
time and are contested.

Not used: *God Is Not Great* (Twelve, 2007). Wikipedia's article on
"Hitchens's razor", citing a Kindle edition, says the book repeats the
sentence about assertion and evidence. That page or chapter could not be
checked against an accessible copy: the Internet Archive copy is restricted
and the Google Books API was over quota. So the book is not cited.
*Letters to a Young Contrarian* was not used for the same reason.

## Selection hypothesis to test

#275 §4: **evidential support and consistency of argument.** The arm should
prefer items whose own source documents the point. It should prefer an item
that tests whether a word applies to a case over one that only asserts it, and
should abstain more often when the evidence is thin. On the same frozen packets
as the chosen baseline (Voltaire v7, and the generic control if D2 ran),
scorecard v2 should show a supported rate no lower than the baseline's and no
more unsupported reasons. If the arm only shifts tone, or rewards combative
headlines, the lens has failed (see the blind spots).
